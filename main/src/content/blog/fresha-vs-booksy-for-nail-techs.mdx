Fresha vs Booksy is the question most nail techs ask once they decide to take bookings online. Both give you a calendar, reminders, deposits and a marketplace. The real difference sits in two places: what the subscription grows with, and what you pay when a brand new client finds you through the app.

A quick disclosure: I build Nailfolio, which is neither of these. It is not a booking app; it sits next to Booksy or Fresha as the portfolio and consultation layer, and I describe it at the end. Everything about Booksy and Fresha below comes from their own pricing and feature pages, **as of September 2026**. Both change terms and both differ by country, so open the pricing page for yours before you decide.

## The short answer

- **You work in Poland:** Fresha is much cheaper on paper (PLN 29 a month solo against Booksy's 145 zł net) and its Polish page lists the marketplace new client fee as free, while Booksy Boost takes 45% net of a new client's first visit.
- **You work in the US or UK and want marketplace clients:** Booksy publishes its Boost rate (30%). Fresha's new client fee depends on your country, so compare both pages in your own currency.
- **Your book is full of regulars:** pick the calendar you like more and keep marketplace fees off. Neither charges for returning clients.
- **You want clients to choose a design from your own work before the visit:** neither does that well. Keep the booking tool and add a portfolio next to it.

## Cost: what the subscription grows with

**Booksy** charges one flat monthly fee that includes every feature, plus a fee for each additional team member:

- **US:** $29.99 per month plus tax, and $20 per month for each additional team member.
- **UK:** £40 per month plus VAT, and £5 per month for each additional team member.
- **Germany:** 35 € per month plus 7 € for each additional person, plus tax.
- **Poland:** 145 zł net per month plus 35 zł net per additional employee, with no extra charge from the tenth employee on.

**Fresha** charges per bookable team member. On the Polish pricing page, the Independent plan for a one-person business is PLN 29 per month, and the Team plan is PLN 19 per bookable team member per month, both before 23% tax, with a 7-day trial. I could not read Fresha's US or UK prices, because its pricing page shows the currency of the country you open it from. Check it from yours.

For a solo nail stylist, the subscription is rarely the deciding number. The marketplace fee is.

## Marketplace and the fee on new clients, by country

Both apps charge **once, on a brand new client who found you through their marketplace**, and never on her later visits. The rules around that fee differ.

### Booksy Boost

Boost is optional and free to turn on. While it is on, a client who books you for the first time through the Booksy marketplace costs a one-time commission:

- **US:** 30% of the total cost of her first visit. Booksy's own Boost page says the tip stays yours.
- **UK:** 30%, with a minimum of £5, plus VAT.
- **Poland:** 45% net of all services from her first completed visit.
- **Germany:** Booksy's German pricing FAQ says it charges no commission and no booking fee for new appointments or new clients.

Booksy's US and UK pages say there is no commission when Boost is off, and none for clients who book through your Booksy profile link or find you through your own marketing, even with Boost on. The Polish page adds that clients already in your Booksy client base are not charged.

### Fresha new client fee

Fresha lists a "one-time fee applied to brand new clients who discover your business via the Fresha marketplace", with no fee on returning clients. On the Polish pricing page that fee reads **free**. I could not read a rate for the US, UK or Germany from my location, so I give no number: it **depends on your country, check the pricing page**. Fresha's FAQ also says you are not charged for new clients who find you through your own website, Instagram or Facebook.

## Payments and no-shows

This is where the two are closest. **Booksy** lets you take a deposit at booking or keep a card on file and charge a cancellation fee, and you decide which services need it and what percentage. Appointment reminders are free. In the US, mobile payments and keyed entry cost 2.69% + $0.30 per transaction.

**Fresha** lists upfront payments and no-show and cancellation fees under payments, plus automated reminders by email and text, with a free text allowance and pay-as-you-go messages after that. On the Polish page, online payments cost 1.29% + PLN 0.49 per transaction. Fresha says you can use the calendar without its payments, but deposits and no-show protection need them.

If no-shows are your biggest loss, either one solves it. Compare the processing rate in your country, because you pay it on every card payment, not once.

## FAQ

### Is Fresha cheaper than Booksy?

In Poland, clearly: PLN 29 against 145 zł net per month for one person, and no new client fee on Fresha's Polish page. Elsewhere it depends on your country and team size, so open both pricing pages in your currency and add up the subscription, the new client fee and card processing.

### Do Booksy and Fresha take a commission on every booking?

No. Booksy charges only on a new client's first visit while Boost is on, and Fresha only on brand new clients from its marketplace. Returning clients cost nothing extra on either.

### Fresha or Booksy for nail techs: which is better?

Neither is built for nails, so decide by money and habit. If you rely on marketplace clients, compare the new client fee in your country. If your book is mostly regulars, pick the calendar and payment rates you prefer.

### Can clients browse my nail designs on Booksy or Fresha?

They can see photos on your profile, but not filter them by color, length or style. For that, pair your booking app with a portfolio that clients can search.
