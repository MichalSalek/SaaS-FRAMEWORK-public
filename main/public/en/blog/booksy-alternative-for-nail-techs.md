# Booksy Alternatives for Nail Techs: What to Switch To

> Thinking of leaving Booksy? Pick a Booksy alternative by your real reason: price, Boost commission, marketplace or portfolio. Honest options for nail stylists.

Most nail techs who search for a Booksy alternative are not angry at the calendar. They are annoyed by one thing: the monthly bill, a commission on a new client, or a tool built for barbers and massage studios that does not think like a nail stylist. Switching helps only if the new tool solves that exact reason.

A quick disclosure: I build Nailfolio, which appears at the end of this post. It is not a booking app and it does not replace Booksy. I describe the other tools from their own public pages, as of September 2026. Prices and terms change and differ by country, so check each vendor before you move.

## Why nail techs look for a Booksy alternative

Name your reason first. Each one points to a different answer.

### The subscription feels expensive

Booksy charges a monthly subscription plus a fee for each additional team member. On its US pricing page that is **$29.99 per month plus tax, and $20 per month for each extra team member**. In the UK it is **£40 per month plus VAT, and £5 per extra team member**. If regulars fill most of your calendar, you may be paying for reach you no longer use.

### The Boost commission

Boost is optional. When it is on, Booksy charges a **one-time commission on the first visit of a new client** who books you for the first time. The rate depends on your country:

- **US:** 30% of the total cost of that first visit.
- **UK:** 30%, with a minimum of £5, plus VAT.
- **Poland:** 45% net of the first completed visit.
- **Germany:** Booksy's German pricing FAQ says it charges no commission on new clients at all.

Booksy's own pages add that clients who book through your personal profile link, or find you through your own marketing, carry no commission.

### Dependence on the marketplace

If most new clients come from the Booksy marketplace, your growth depends on Booksy's ranking and terms. Some stylists leave to build their own channel: Instagram, Google, referrals and a booking link they control.

### The portfolio does not help clients choose

A booking profile shows your work as a set of photos a client cannot filter by color, length or style, so the design conversation still happens at the chair, often over a Pinterest screenshot.

### It is too generic

Booksy serves barbers, hair salons, spas, massage and nails. If you want a tool that thinks in nail terms, that outweighs price.

## Fresha

Fresha is a booking, payments and marketplace platform for beauty and wellness. It prices per bookable team member, with an Independent plan for one person and a Team plan above it. Like Booksy, it charges a **one-time fee on brand-new clients who discover you through the Fresha marketplace**, and never on returning clients. How much that fee is depends on the country, so "Booksy vs Fresha" has no single answer.

- **Solves:** the subscription cost or new-client fee, in regions where Fresha's is lower.
- **You give up:** nothing nail-specific. Fresha is as broad as Booksy.

## GlossGenius

GlossGenius is a US platform for booking, payments and a branded booking website. Its pricing page lists Standard at $28 per month ($24 billed annually), Gold at $56 ($48 annually) and Platinum at $168 ($148 annually), a flat 2.6% card processing rate, a booking website with a portfolio image gallery, and a 14-day trial with no free plan.

- **Solves:** a polished, own-brand booking page and simple payment pricing.
- **You give up:** I found no consumer marketplace on its pricing page, so new clients come from your own channels. US only.

## Vagaro

Vagaro covers booking, payments, marketing and salon management for beauty and wellness, and every plan includes a listing on the Vagaro Marketplace. Its price scales with the number of bookable calendars, so check the pricing page for your country and team size.

- **Solves:** a broad toolset for a growing salon, with marketplace exposure.
- **You give up:** simplicity, and again, a nail-specific approach.

## Square Appointments

Square Appointments is the booking side of Square payments. As of September 2026, its US pricing page lists a **free plan at $0 per month per location** for solo professionals, with an online booking site, reminders and unlimited staff calendars. Plus is $49 and Premium $149 per location per month; cancellation policies with no-show fees and waitlists are listed with Plus. Card processing fees apply on every plan.

- **Solves:** the subscription, if the free plan covers you, especially if you already use Square payments.
- **You give up:** no-show fees on the free plan, and marketplace discovery on the scale of Booksy.

## Nailfies

Nailfies describes itself as an all-in-one booking and portfolio platform for nail professionals: you manage clients, show your designs and offer AI try-ons, and clients need no app download. I could not find public pricing on its site.

- **Solves:** the "too generic" problem, with a tool made only for nails.
- **You give up:** check pricing, your country and how the portfolio is organized before you move your bookings.

## A simple calendar with no marketplace

If your book is full of regulars, you may not need a marketplace at all. A plain online scheduler with reminders and a link in your Instagram bio can be enough.

- **Solves:** commissions and complexity.
- **You give up:** new-client discovery, and beauty-specific features like deposits on some tools.

## FAQ

### How do I avoid Booksy's commission?

Booksy's commission applies only while Boost is on, and only to a new client's first visit. Booksy's US and UK pages say clients who book through your personal profile link or your own marketing carry no commission, so share that link everywhere. Or turn Boost off. Terms differ by country, so read Booksy's current terms for yours.

### Is Fresha cheaper than Booksy?

It depends on your country. Both charge a subscription that grows with your team, and both can charge a one-time fee on marketplace clients, at rates that vary by region. Open both pricing pages in your currency and compare the total for your team size.

### Can I move my clients from Booksy?

Usually, with some manual work. Export your client list if your plan allows it, and ask the new tool about import or data transfer (GlossGenius advertises a free one). Send regulars your new booking link before you cancel.

### What is the best Booksy alternative for a solo nail tech?

The one that solves your reason for leaving. For cost, compare Fresha or Square's free plan in your country. For commission, a simple calendar with your own link. For a nail-specific tool, look at Nailfies. If the booking side works and the consultation does not, keep Booksy and add a portfolio next to it.
