# Best Nail Portfolio Apps for Nail Techs in 2026

> An honest look at nail portfolio apps in 2026: Nailfolio, Nailfies, Booksy, Fresha, GlossGenius and Instagram, what each does best and which pair to pick.

HTML version of this page: https://nailfolio.app/en/blog/best-nail-portfolio-apps-2026/ | Published: 2026-09-23 | Category: Tips for Stylists

Search for the best nail portfolio app and most lists you find rank booking software. That is useful if your problem is an empty calendar. It does not help if your problem is the start of every visit, spent scrolling Instagram with a client who is holding up a Pinterest photo.

A quick disclosure: I build Nailfolio, one of the apps below, so weigh what I say about it accordingly. I describe the other tools from their own public pages, as of September 2026. Features and prices change, so check each vendor before you decide.

## The short answer

- **You need more bookings:** a booking app with a marketplace, such as Booksy or Fresha.
- **You want booking and a portfolio in one nail-specific tool:** look at Nailfies.
- **You work in the US and care about a polished booking page and payments:** GlossGenius or Vagaro.
- **You want clients to pick from your real work before they sit down, and to remember what you did for each of them:** a portfolio with filters and client history, such as Nailfolio, next to whatever you use for bookings.
- **You want reach:** keep Instagram, but stop using the grid as the place where clients choose a design.

## Three jobs that get mixed up

Most "best app" debates go in circles because three different jobs get lumped together:

1. **Getting booked:** online booking, reminders, deposits and no-show protection.
2. **Getting discovered:** new clients finding you through Instagram or a marketplace.
3. **Choosing the design and remembering the client:** she picks from what you actually do before the appointment, and you can see what you agreed and what you did at her last visits.

Booking apps are excellent at the first job and decent at the second. The third usually lives in your camera roll and your DMs.

## The tools, one by one

### Nailfolio

Every photo in your gallery carries a color, a style and a length, and clients filter your own work with all three at once. A permanent QR code at your station opens your public profile; the client signs in without a password, sends a connection request, and sees your full gallery once you accept it with one tap. Before the visit, she collects her picks in an Inspiration Session with a note on each photo and sends it to you. You talk in Messages, and her client card keeps her choices and the history of recent visits.

- **Best for:** stylists who lose the start of each visit to phone scrolling and want clients to arrive with a choice made from their real work.
- **Watch out for:** Nailfolio does not take bookings or send reminders, so you pair it with a booking tool. It is built in Poland and paid plans are billed in PLN. It works in the browser on any phone, iPhone included; the Android app is coming soon to Google Play. Clients always use it for free, and there is a Free plan for stylists. Details on the [pricing page](/en/pricing).

### Nailfies

Nailfies describes itself as an all-in-one booking and portfolio platform for nail professionals: you manage clients, showcase designs and offer AI try-ons, with no app download needed for clients.

- **Best for:** nail techs who want booking and a design showcase in one tool built for nails.
- **Watch out for:** check how the portfolio is organized (whether clients can filter by length and style) and how pricing works in your country before you move your bookings.

### Booksy

Booksy is a booking app with a large consumer marketplace. Your booking profile shows your services and portfolio photos, and you get reminders and no-show protection. It runs on a monthly subscription. Its Boost feature promotes you in the marketplace and charges a one-time commission on the first visit of a new client who found you there; the rate depends on your country, so read Booksy's current terms.

- **Best for:** filling your calendar with new clients.
- **Watch out for:** portfolio photos on a booking profile cannot be filtered by color or length, so the design conversation still happens at the chair.

### Fresha

Fresha combines booking, payments and a marketplace, on a subscription. In some countries it charges a one-time fee on new clients who book you through the Fresha marketplace; in others that fee is zero, so check your region.

- **Best for:** an affordable calendar with online payments and marketplace exposure.
- **Watch out for:** it serves all of beauty and wellness, so photos of your work are an add-on to your profile rather than a portfolio clients browse.

### GlossGenius and Vagaro

GlossGenius is a US platform for booking, payments and a branded booking website, on paid plans. Vagaro covers booking and salon management for beauty and wellness and has its own marketplace.

- **Best for:** US stylists who want payments, marketing tools and a professional booking page in one place.
- **Watch out for:** as with the other booking tools, the portfolio is there to support booking, not to let a client narrow your work down to what fits her nails.

### Instagram and Pinterest

Instagram is free and still a common place to be discovered. As a portfolio it has two problems: the grid is chronological, so last year's perfect match is hundreds of posts down, and there is no way to filter by color, length or style. What you agree with a client then gets scattered across DMs.

Pinterest is where clients find ideas, but those are strangers' nails. That is why clients arrive with long almond sets when they wear their nails short and square.

- **Best for:** reach and inspiration.
- **Watch out for:** neither one shows a client what you can do on her nails.

## Which combination to pick

**You already use a booking app and it works.** Keep it. Add a portfolio with filters and client history next to it, so clients book through your usual link and arrive with a design picked from your own work.

**Your bookings happen in Instagram DMs.** Decide what hurts more. If it is no-shows, start with a booking tool that takes deposits. If it is the long design conversation, start with a portfolio clients can filter on their own before the appointment.

**You run a salon with a team.** Keep the team calendar in your booking system and put the shared gallery and client knowledge somewhere that stays with the salon when a stylist leaves. In Nailfolio that is the Studio plan, with accounts for up to 10 people on the team included: [Nailfolio for salons](/en/for-studios).

## FAQ

### Can I use Instagram as my nail portfolio?

As a place to be found, yes. As the place where a client picks her design, it works poorly, because the grid cannot be filtered by color, length or style and older work disappears under new posts.

### Do I need a separate portfolio app if I use Booksy?

Only if the design conversation takes too long or you keep forgetting what you did for each client. Booksy handles booking well; a portfolio app with filters handles the choice before the visit. They work side by side.

### What should a nail portfolio show?

Your own work, photographed in the same light, and tagged so a client can find it: color, length, shape and style. Pin a handful of your strongest designs where a new client looks first.

### Is there a free nail portfolio app?

Instagram is free. Nailfolio has a Free plan for stylists with no card and no time limit, with a limited number of photos and connected clients. Booking apps usually offer a free trial before a paid subscription.
